Some people can only study in total silence. Others can't concentrate without rain sounds or a lofi playlist. Neither is wrong. What matters is matching the sound to the task and to you.

This guide walks through the most popular options, what they're good for, and how to test what works best for your brain.

## Why background sound can help

A quiet room isn't always quiet. Housemates talking, traffic, a ticking clock: unpredictable sounds grab your attention because your brain is wired to notice change. A steady background sound **masks** those interruptions, giving your attention fewer reasons to wander.

Sound can also act as a **cue**. If you always play the same ambience when you study, your brain starts to associate it with focus, and simply pressing play helps you slip into work mode.

## The options

### 🌧️ Rain, fireplaces and nature sounds

Steady, natural ambience is the safest choice for most people. It's predictable, has no words, and covers background noise well. It works for almost any task, including reading and writing, which are easily disrupted by lyrics.

*Try:* [Rain on Window](/videos/rain-on-window-8-hours) for long days, or [Library with Fireplace, Thunderstorm and Rain](/videos/library-fireplace-thunderstorm) for cozy dark-academia vibes. The [workspace](/workspace) also has a built-in rain generator (the cloud icon) that needs no internet.

### 🎵 Lofi and instrumental music

Lofi hip-hop, soft jazz and video-game soundtracks are hugely popular for studying, and many people find they lift their mood and make long sessions feel lighter. The key is **no lyrics** for anything language-heavy: words in music compete with the words you're trying to read or write.

Music tends to suit repetitive or practical tasks (problem sets, organising notes, making flashcards) better than tasks that need careful reading.

*Try:* [Winter Jazz](/videos/winter-jazz-60-10) or [Animal Crossing Ambience](/videos/animal-crossing-ambience).

### 🏙️ Study-with-me and scenic pomodoro videos

These combine ambience or music with a built-in timer and a calm view. Some people find the sense of "studying alongside someone" motivating, a bit like working in a library. They're especially handy if you like the structure of a set rhythm.

*Try:* [Yokohama Harbour (25/5)](/videos/yokohama-harbour-25-5) or [Shinjuku (50/10)](/videos/shinjuku-50-10).

### 🎧 Binaural beats and 40 Hz audio

Binaural beats play a slightly different tone in each ear (you need headphones), and your brain perceives a "beat" at the difference between them. 40 Hz tracks are often marketed as boosting focus or "gamma waves".

To be honest about it: **the research is mixed and still developing.** Some studies report small effects on attention or mood; others find little difference. They're unlikely to hurt, so if you're curious, treat them as an experiment: try a few sessions and see how you feel. If they give you a headache or feel distracting, plain rain works just as well.

*Try:* [Binaural Beats 40 Hz](/videos/binaural-beats-40hz) or [40 Hz Gamma](/videos/40hz-gamma-waves).

### 🤫 Silence

Don't overlook it. For the hardest work, like learning brand-new material, tricky maths or close reading, many people concentrate best with no sound at all. Foam earplugs or noise-cancelling headphones with nothing playing can be surprisingly powerful.

## Quick guide: what to play for which task

| Task | Good choices |
| --- | --- |
| Reading & note-taking | Silence, rain, nature ambience |
| Essay writing | Rain, fireplace, very soft instrumental |
| Maths & problem sets | Lofi, instrumental, ambience |
| Flashcards & memorising | Ambience or silence |
| Organising, admin, tidying notes | Any music you enjoy |
| Winding down after studying | A short [guided meditation](/videos?category=meditation) |

## Keep the volume low

Background sound should sit *in the background*. If you notice yourself listening to it, it's too loud or too interesting. Turn it down until it's just enough to cover the room.

## Build your own focus cue

Pick one sound for your most important study sessions and use it **only** for focused work. Over a couple of weeks, that sound becomes a trigger: press play, and your brain gets the message that it's time to work.
